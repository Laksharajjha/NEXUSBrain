# NEXUS-0: A From-Scratch, Inspectable Learning System

## 1. Overview and Research Question

NEXUS-0 is an experimental research prototype investigating whether a symbolic, inspectable learning system can acquire language and concepts from perceptual experience without large language models, transformers, pretrained embeddings, or external model APIs.

### Core Research Question
> *Can a system induce word meanings and grammatical constructions from (scene, sentence) pairs, generalize to held-out combinations, and restructure its own concepts when evidence demands it, better than simpler baselines?*

### Principles & Hard Constraints
- **Zero Pretrained Weights:** Standard Python 3.11+ and NumPy only for core computation.
- **Strictly Deterministic:** Every run is parameterized by an explicit seed. Sub-seeds are derived via SHA-256 to ensure complete platform independence.
- **Traceable Beliefs:** Every learned item has provenance: what was learned, from which experiences, by which mechanism, and why behavior changed.
- **Transparent Inductive Bias:** The learner's hypothesis space and structural assumptions are explicitly documented rather than disguised as "learning from nothing."
- **Fail Loudly:** No silent fallbacks or heuristics on malformed input.

## 2. World and Generation Mechanism (Phase 0)

### 2.1 Perceptual Ontology
A scene consists of 2 to 4 objects and optional binary relations:
- **Kinds (6):** `cube`, `ball`, `cone`, `ring`, `star`, `cup`
- **Colors (4):** `red`, `green`, `blue`, `yellow`
- **Sizes (3):** `small`, `medium`, `large`
- **Relations (2):** `chases` (directed, asymmetric), `next_to` (symmetric)

Objects are represented symbolically by their perceptual attribute values:
$$\text{Object}(i) = \langle \text{kind}, \text{color}, \text{size} \rangle$$

### 2.2 Hidden Grammar
Sentences are generated by a hidden grammar using invented vocabulary and non-English syntactic order (configured in `nexus/world/configs/grammar_default.json`):
- **Noun Phrase Order:** `[noun, size, color]` (e.g. `dax pim tal` = `cube small green`, adjectives follow nouns).
- **Clause Word Order:** Subject - Object - Verb (SOV) for transitive sentences.
- **Questions:** Marked with a sentence-final question particle `ko` (yes/no query).
- **Invented Lexicon:**
  - Kinds: `dax` (cube), `wug` (ball), `blick` (cone), `toma` (ring), `fep` (star), `zib` (cup)
  - Colors: `mip` (red), `tal` (green), `sorn` (blue), `vek` (yellow)
  - Sizes: `pim` (small), `glorp` (medium), `nud` (large)
  - Relations: `zorp` (chases), `lum` (next_to)
  - Question Particle: `ko`

The grammar defines four constructions:
1. `np_assert`: e.g. `dax mip` ("A red cube exists.")
2. `np_ask`: e.g. `dax mip ko` ("Is there a red cube?")
3. `trans_assert`: e.g. `dax wug zorp` ("A cube chases a ball.")
4. `trans_ask`: e.g. `dax wug zorp ko` ("Does a cube chase a ball?")

### 2.3 Meaning-First Generation and Contrastive Pairs
Data generation operates meaning-first:
1. A structured semantic specification is sampled.
2. The hidden grammar realizes it into a sequence of surface tokens and an existential first-order logical form (LF):
   $$\text{ACT}(\exists x_0 \dots x_n . \text{atom}_1 \wedge \dots \wedge \text{atom}_k)$$
3. A witness scene making the sentence true is constructed with random distractors.
4. **Contrastive Pairs:** Because positive-only data leaves word meanings underdetermined, the generator produces near-miss contrastive scenes (identical scene except for a single attribute or relation edit that makes the sentence false).
5. Ground-truth truth values are evaluated by a first-order model checker over the scene objects.

## 3. Evaluation Harness and Split Design

Each dataset split is strictly isolated to test distinct facets of generalization. Held-out assignments (novel noun, held-out adjective-noun pairs, held-out grammatical roles, contradiction target words) are chosen per seed by a `HeldOutPlan`.

| Split Name | Purpose | Design Constraints |
|---|---|---|
| `train` | Primary learning curriculum | At most 1 adjective per NP. Excludes the novel noun, held-out combinations, and stacked adjectives. Grouped by curriculum stages (`np_*` then `trans_*`). |
| `dev` | In-distribution validation | Same constraints as train, stratified by construction, unseen (scene, sentence) pairs. Used for learning curves and retention. |
| `test_novel_combination` | Compositional generalization | Known words in unseen combinations (e.g. held-out `color + kind` or held-out `kind @ subj/obj` role). Zero token-sequence overlap with train. |
| `test_novel_word_in_known_frame` | One-shot lexical acquisition | A noun never seen during training. Provided with a support stream of 1 contrastive pair (2 examples), then tested across other constructions. |
| `test_longer` | Length / structural extrapolation | Sentences strictly longer than any training sentence (multiple stacked adjectives per NP). |
| `test_contradiction` | Belief revision & retraction | Support stream where a teacher systematically mislabels an adjective, followed by corrective feedback. Evaluated on diagnostic probes. |

### Structural Disjointness Assertions
The test suite explicitly enforces that:
- $\text{item} = (\text{tokens}, \text{canonical\_scene})$ in any test split has **zero intersection** with `train`.
- Test splits have exactly $50\%$ True and $50\%$ False ground-truth balance.
- Canonical scene representations are invariant to object permutations and IDs.

## 4. Inductive Bias & Learner Hypothesis Space

To ensure scientific honesty, the inductive biases and hypothesis spaces are explicitly delineated:

### Perceptual Primitives (Given to Learner)
- The learner receives object vectors decomposed into attribute slots: `kind`, `color`, `size`.
- The learner perceives directed relation links between object indices.
- *The learner does NOT receive:* word meanings, grammatical roles, syntactic trees, or logical forms.

### Lexical & Grammatical Hypothesis Space (Phases 1–3)
- **Word Meanings:** A word maps to a predicate over an attribute value (unary) or a relation (binary).
- **Cross-Situational Evidence:** The learner maintains co-occurrence counts and Bayesian posterior scores between words and perceptual features across scenes.
- **Composition:** Syntactic categories and combination rules are induced from data; competing hypotheses are scored and pruned by minimum description length (MDL) or posterior probability.

## 5. Metrics

1. **Accuracy:** Fraction of items where $\text{predicted\_truth} == \text{ground\_truth}$.
2. **Expected Calibration Error (ECE):**
   $$\text{ECE} = \sum_{b=1}^B \frac{|B_b|}{N} \left| \text{acc}(B_b) - \text{conf}(B_b) \right|$$
   binned into equal-width intervals over confidence $[0.5, 1.0]$.
3. **Brier Score:** Mean squared error between predicted probability $p(\text{true})$ and binary outcome:
   $$\text{Brier} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (p_i - y_i)^2$$
4. **Logical Form Accuracy:** Exact match of predicted logical form (when parser is active).
5. **Examples-to-Criterion:** Total training examples seen before dev accuracy on a construction reaches the criterion threshold ($\ge 0.90$).
6. **Retention:** Accuracy on early constructions at the end of their stage vs. at the end of training ($\Delta = \text{acc}_{\text{final}} - \text{acc}_{\text{stage\_end}}$).
7. **Model Size:** Number of active hypothesis entries or parameters.

## 6. Phase 0 Results: Baseline A (Memorizer)

Baseline A is a pure lookup table with Laplace-smoothed backoff:
1. Exact match on $(\text{tokens}, \text{canonical\_scene})$
2. Sentence match on $\text{tokens}$
3. Global prior

Running 20 seeds through `nexus.experiments.phase0_memorizer`:

### Accuracy and Calibration (20 seeds, mean ± std)

| Split | Accuracy | ECE | Brier | Mean Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `train` | **1.000 ± 0.000** | 0.333 ± 0.000 | 0.111 ± 0.000 | 0.667 ± 0.000 |
| `dev` | **0.493 ± 0.021** | 0.049 ± 0.015 | 0.254 ± 0.003 | 0.536 ± 0.004 |
| `test_novel_combination` | **0.500 ± 0.000** | 0.006 ± 0.004 | 0.250 ± 0.000 | 0.506 ± 0.004 |
| `test_novel_word_in_known_frame` | **0.500 ± 0.000** | 0.006 ± 0.004 | 0.250 ± 0.000 | 0.506 ± 0.004 |
| `test_longer` | **0.500 ± 0.000** | 0.006 ± 0.004 | 0.250 ± 0.000 | 0.506 ± 0.004 |
| `test_contradiction` | **0.500 ± 0.000** | 0.005 ± 0.005 | 0.250 ± 0.000 | 0.505 ± 0.005 |

### Support-Stream Checkpoints (Accuracy)

| Split | Checkpoint | Memorizer Accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| `test_novel_word_in_known_frame` | `before_support` | 0.500 ± 0.000 |
| `test_novel_word_in_known_frame` | `after_support` | 0.500 ± 0.000 |
| `test_contradiction` | `before_stream` | 0.500 ± 0.000 |
| `test_contradiction` | `before_corrections` | 0.500 ± 0.000 |
| `test_contradiction` | `after_corrections` | 0.500 ± 0.000 |

### Verification of the Evaluation Harness
- **Memorizer learns training data perfectly:** $100.0\%$ accuracy on `train`.
- **Zero test leakage:** On all held-out splits (`test_novel_combination`, `test_novel_word_in_known_frame`, `test_longer`, `test_contradiction`), the memorizer performs at exactly chance ($50.0\%$).
- **No one-shot generalization:** Seeing the novel noun in the support pair provides zero transfer to other constructions under exact matching.
- This confirms that any above-chance performance in Phase 1 requires genuine grammatical and semantic generalization.

## 8. Known Limitations (Phase 0) & Next Steps (Phase 1)

### Phase 0 Limitations
- The memorizer cannot decompose phrases into words; it has no notion of semantic composition.
- Scenes are limited to 2–4 objects with at most 2 binary relations.
- Adjectives are currently restricted to unary properties (`color`, `size`).

### Next Steps: Phase 1 (NEXUS Grammar + Meaning Induction)
1. **Cross-Situational Lexicon Induction:** Track co-occurrence distributions between surface words and scene features.
2. **Grammar Induction:** Categorial grammar induction algorithm proposing lexical entries and semantic composition operators.
3. **Hypothesis Scorer & Pruner:** Bayesian posterior / description length scoring over candidate grammars without premature pruning.
4. **Explainability:** `explain(sentence)` providing the full derivation tree and experiential provenance for each rule used.
5. **Baselines B & C:** Implement cross-situational-only learner (no grammar) and no-revision grammar learner.
