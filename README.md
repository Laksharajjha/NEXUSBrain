# NEXUS-0: A From-Scratch, Inspectable Learning System

## 1. Overview and Research Question

NEXUS-0 is an experimental research prototype investigating whether a symbolic, inspectable learning system can acquire language and concepts from perceptual experience without large language models, transformers, pretrained embeddings, or external model APIs.

### Core Research Question
> *Can a system induce word meanings and grammatical constructions from (scene, sentence) pairs, generalize to held-out combinations, and restructure its own concepts when evidence demands it, better than simpler baselines?*

### Principles & Hard Constraints
- **Zero Pretrained Weights:** Standard Python 3.11+ and NumPy only for core computation.
- **Strictly Deterministic:** Every run is parameterized by an explicit seed. Sub-seeds are derived via SHA-256 to ensure complete platform independence.
- **Traceable Beliefs:** Every learned item has provenance: what was learned, from which experiences, by which mechanism, and why behavior changed.
- **Transparent Inductive Bias:** The learner's hypothesis space and structural assumptions are explicitly documented rather than disguised as "learning from nothing."
- **Fail Loudly:** No silent fallbacks or heuristics on malformed input.

## 2. World and Generation Mechanism (Phase 0)

### 2.1 Perceptual Ontology
A scene consists of 2 to 4 objects and optional binary relations:
- **Kinds (6):** `cube`, `ball`, `cone`, `ring`, `star`, `cup`
- **Colors (4):** `red`, `green`, `blue`, `yellow`
- **Sizes (3):** `small`, `medium`, `large`
- **Relations (2):** `chases` (directed, asymmetric), `next_to` (symmetric)

Objects are represented symbolically by their perceptual attribute values:
$$\text{Object}(i) = \langle \text{kind}, \text{color}, \text{size} \rangle$$

### 2.2 Hidden Grammar
Sentences are generated by a hidden grammar using invented vocabulary and non-English syntactic order (configured in `nexus/world/configs/grammar_default.json`):
- **Noun Phrase Order:** `[noun, size, color]` (e.g. `dax pim tal` = `cube small green`, adjectives follow nouns).
- **Clause Word Order:** Subject - Object - Verb (SOV) for transitive sentences.
- **Questions:** Marked with a sentence-final question particle `ko` (yes/no query).
- **Invented Lexicon:**
  - Kinds: `dax` (cube), `wug` (ball), `blick` (cone), `toma` (ring), `fep` (star), `zib` (cup)
  - Colors: `mip` (red), `tal` (green), `sorn` (blue), `vek` (yellow)
  - Sizes: `pim` (small), `glorp` (medium), `nud` (large)
  - Relations: `zorp` (chases), `lum` (next_to)
  - Question Particle: `ko`

The grammar defines four constructions:
1. `np_assert`: e.g. `dax mip` ("A red cube exists.")
2. `np_ask`: e.g. `dax mip ko` ("Is there a red cube?")
3. `trans_assert`: e.g. `dax wug zorp` ("A cube chases a ball.")
4. `trans_ask`: e.g. `dax wug zorp ko` ("Does a cube chase a ball?")

### 2.3 Meaning-First Generation and Contrastive Pairs
Data generation operates meaning-first:
1. A structured semantic specification is sampled.
2. The hidden grammar realizes it into a sequence of surface tokens and an existential first-order logical form (LF):
   $$\text{ACT}(\exists x_0 \dots x_n . \text{atom}_1 \wedge \dots \wedge \text{atom}_k)$$
3. A witness scene making the sentence true is constructed with random distractors.
4. **Contrastive Pairs:** Because positive-only data leaves word meanings underdetermined, the generator produces near-miss contrastive scenes (identical scene except for a single attribute or relation edit that makes the sentence false).
5. Ground-truth truth values are evaluated by a first-order model checker over the scene objects.

## 3. Evaluation Harness and Split Design

Each dataset split is strictly isolated to test distinct facets of generalization. Held-out assignments (novel noun, held-out adjective-noun pairs, held-out grammatical roles, contradiction target words) are chosen per seed by a `HeldOutPlan`.

| Split Name | Purpose | Design Constraints |
|---|---|---|
| `train` | Primary learning curriculum | At most 1 adjective per NP. Excludes the novel noun, held-out combinations, and stacked adjectives. Grouped by curriculum stages (`np_*` then `trans_*`). |
| `dev` | In-distribution validation | Same constraints as train, stratified by construction, unseen (scene, sentence) pairs. Used for learning curves and retention. |
| `test_novel_combination` | Compositional generalization | Known words in unseen combinations (e.g. held-out `color + kind` or held-out `kind @ subj/obj` role). Zero token-sequence overlap with train. |
| `test_novel_word_in_known_frame` | One-shot lexical acquisition | A noun never seen during training. Provided with a support stream of 1 contrastive pair (2 examples), then tested across other constructions. |
| `test_longer` | Length / structural extrapolation | Sentences strictly longer than any training sentence (multiple stacked adjectives per NP). |
| `test_contradiction` | Belief revision & retraction | Support stream where a teacher systematically mislabels an adjective, followed by corrective feedback. Evaluated on diagnostic probes. |

### Structural Disjointness Assertions
The test suite explicitly enforces that:
- $\text{item} = (\text{tokens}, \text{canonical\_scene})$ in any test split has **zero intersection** with `train`.
- Test splits have exactly $50\%$ True and $50\%$ False ground-truth balance.
- Canonical scene representations are invariant to object permutations and IDs.

## 4. Inductive Bias & Learner Hypothesis Space

To ensure scientific honesty, the inductive biases and hypothesis spaces are explicitly delineated:

### Perceptual Primitives (Given to Learner)
- The learner receives object vectors decomposed into attribute slots: `kind`, `color`, `size`.
- The learner perceives directed relation links between object indices.
- *The learner does NOT receive:* word meanings, grammatical roles, syntactic trees, or logical forms.

### Lexical & Grammatical Hypothesis Space (Phases 1–3)
- **Word Meanings:** A word maps to a predicate over an attribute value (unary) or a relation (binary).
- **Cross-Situational Evidence:** The learner maintains co-occurrence counts and Bayesian posterior scores between words and perceptual features across scenes.
- **Composition:** Syntactic categories and combination rules are induced from data; competing hypotheses are scored and pruned by minimum description length (MDL) or posterior probability.

## 5. Metrics

1. **Accuracy:** Fraction of items where $\text{predicted\_truth} == \text{ground\_truth}$.
2. **Expected Calibration Error (ECE):**
   $$\text{ECE} = \sum_{b=1}^B \frac{|B_b|}{N} \left| \text{acc}(B_b) - \text{conf}(B_b) \right|$$
   binned into equal-width intervals over confidence $[0.5, 1.0]$.
3. **Brier Score:** Mean squared error between predicted probability $p(\text{true})$ and binary outcome:
   $$\text{Brier} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (p_i - y_i)^2$$
4. **Logical Form Accuracy:** Exact match of predicted logical form (when parser is active).
5. **Examples-to-Criterion:** Total training examples seen before dev accuracy on a construction reaches the criterion threshold ($\ge 0.90$).
6. **Retention:** Accuracy on early constructions at the end of their stage vs. at the end of training ($\Delta = \text{acc}_{\text{final}} - \text{acc}_{\text{stage\_end}}$).
7. **Model Size:** Number of active hypothesis entries or parameters.

### 7.2 Diagnosed and Resolved Artifacts
1. **Novel Kind Distractor Leakage (Bug A):** In Phase 0, `train` sentences excluded `novel_kind`, but `true_scene` distractors and `random_false_scene` sampled from the entire ontology including `novel_kind`. **Fix:** Restricted scene distractor sampling in train/dev/longer strictly to `known_kinds`.
2. **Relation Count & Graph Topology Disparity (Bug B):** Phase 0 deleted relations and Phase 0.5 swapped relation endpoints on single relations (introducing 2-cycles `topo:2_cycle` in False scenes) and sampled uniform replacement kinds (causing held-out selection bias on `test_novel_combination` and `test_longer`). **Fix:** Multi-predicate scenes are falsified via attribute/role `binding_edits` that strictly preserve object counts, all feature counts, relation counts, and directed relation graph topology (`topo:*`), while value-replacement edits use symmetric $2\times 2$ crossover quads (`value_swap_quad_scenes`).

## 8. Verification Notes (Task 4 & Stage A7)

### 8.1 Note (a): Memorizer Dev Accuracy & Even Per-Construction Dev Count
- **Intended Overlap:** The intended overlap between `dev` and `train` is strictly **zero exact (scene, tokens) pairs**. Dev represents in-distribution generalization to novel scenes using seen grammatical constructions.
- **Even Per-Construction Dev Count:** `n_dev_per_construction` is enforced to be even by `ConfigError` validation (`100` per construction in `world_default.json`, `n=400` total; `30` per construction in `small_config`, `n=120` total).
- **Explanation:** In Phase 0, when `True` and `False` items in `dev` were sampled with independent sentences, a sentence seen in `train` with a single `False` scene caused the memorizer's sentence-level backoff (`by_sentence[tokens]`) to predict `False` on an unseen `dev` item with that sentence, yielding $0.493 \pm 0.021$. With paired contrastive balancing in Phase 0.6 (`_balanced_items`), every `dev` sentence appears once with a `True` scene and once with a `False` scene, so sentence-level backoff yields **exact chance ($0.500 \pm 0.000$)**.

### 8.2 Note (b): Structural Definition of `test_longer` & Unidentifiability of Relative Modifier Order
- **Definition:** `test_longer` is **not** merely recombining known words within the training sentence length ($L \le 6$). Structurally, every item in `test_longer` features **multiple modifiers (both a size AND a color adjective)** qualifying NPs so that total utterance length $L > L_{\text{train}}$ (where training NPs have `max_adjectives_train = 1`, i.e., **zero training NPs contain both a size and a color modifier simultaneously**, `n_train_stacked_nps = 0`).
- **Honest Scope of Learning:** Because no single training NP contains both a `size` and a `color` modifier at the same time, the relative surface order *between* `size` and `color` inside a 2-adjective NP is **not** identifiable from `train` and is never claimed to be learned. What *is* identifiable from `train` (with $293.0 \pm 18.7$ distinguishing examples per seed) is whether modifiers precede or follow their head noun (head directionality / modifier attachment), which suffices to attach stacked modifiers to the right head noun in `test_longer` since conjunction (`size(x) & color(x)`) is commutative.
- **Verification:** Unit test `test_test_longer_structurally_exceeds_training` asserts that $\forall e \in \text{test\_longer}, \text{len}(e.\text{tokens}) > \text{max\_train\_length}$.

### 8.3 Note (c): One-Shot Support Stream Composition & Truth-Value Supervision
- **Composition:** The support stream for `test_novel_word_in_known_frame` consists of 1 contrastive bare-NP pair (**2 examples**, evaluated at `before_support` and `after_support`).
- **Structure & Feedback:**
  - Example 1 (`label=True`): A bare-NP observation featuring the novel noun (e.g. `zib` or `ko zib`) paired with a witness scene $S_{\text{true}}$ containing 1 object of `novel_kind`.
  - Example 2 (`label=False`): The *identical* sentence paired with a near-miss contrastive scene $S_{\text{false}}$ where the `novel_kind` object's `kind` attribute is replaced by another kind while all colors, sizes, and relations remain unchanged.
  - Both events are standard `observe` events (no `Correction` retraction events), and **both labels (`True` and `False`) provide binary truth-value supervision**. As verified in `AUDIT_0_6.md` (Section A3), within the 15-predicate Phase 1 hypothesis space, this 2-example support stream leaves **exactly 1 consistent candidate (`novel_kind`)** across all 20 seeds, giving an ideal-observer accuracy ceiling of **1.000**.

### 8.4 Note (d): Correction Semantics in `test_contradiction`
- **Definition:** In `test_contradiction`, corrupted initial examples assert an erroneous attribute mapping (e.g. `tal` $\to$ `red` instead of `green`). During the correction stream, an explicit `Correction` event is supplied:
  $$\text{Correction}(\text{target\_example\_id}, \text{corrected\_label}=\text{opposite}, \text{reason}="teacher\_error")$$
- A **correct response** requires:
  1. Retracting the corrupted belief on diagnostic probes involving the affected words.
  2. Leaving unaffected beliefs unchanged (no catastrophic forgetting).
- **Metric:** Evaluated at `after_corrections`. The Oracle baseline demonstrates that the harness metric can cleanly reach **100% accuracy (1.000 ± 0.000)** on this split.

### 8.5 Note (e): Expressivity Limits of Logical Forms
- **Expressivity:** Ground-truth logical forms are strictly existential conjunctions of unary and binary predicates:
  $$\text{ACT}(\exists x_0 \dots x_n . \bigwedge_i P_i(x_j) \wedge \bigwedge_k R_k(x_a, x_b))$$
- **Unclaimable Results:**
  - Cannot claim logical deduction, Boolean algebra, or syllogistic reasoning.
  - Cannot claim quantifier handling ($\forall$, "most", "at least two") or quantifier scope ambiguity.
  - Cannot claim reasoning with negation ($\neg$), disjunction ($\lor$), or counterfactuals.
  Claims are strictly bounded to **perceptually grounded predicate induction and syntactic-semantic composition**.

### 8.6 Note (f): Meaninglessness of Training-Split ECE
- Training-split Expected Calibration Error (ECE) is not a meaningful metric of predictive uncertainty.
- For memorizers or unregularized learners, training accuracy is $100\%$ while Laplace smoothing yields probabilities around $2/3 \approx 0.667$, producing an artificial ECE penalty of $|1.0 - 0.667| = 0.333$.
- Calibration is statistically meaningful only on held-out generalization splits (`dev` and `test_*`).

## 9. Phase 1 Learner Architecture & Evaluation Summary

### 9.1 Categorial Grammar & MDL Beam Learner (`nexus/learner/`)
- **Syntactic Hypothesis Space (`nexus/learner/categorial.py`)**:
  - Categorial grammar over base categories `{N, NP, S}` with directional slashes (`/` and `\`) of depth $\le 2$:
    - Depth 0: `N`, `NP`, `S`
    - Depth 1: `N/N` (pre-nominal modifier), `N\N` (post-nominal modifier), `S/S` (initial question marker), `S\S` (final question marker)
    - Depth 2: `(S\NP)\NP` (SOV verb), `(S\NP)/NP` (SVO verb), `(S/NP)/NP` (VSO verb)
  - Composition uses binary Forward Application (`>`) and Backward Application (`<`) only. No NP or clause word-order templates are encoded; word order emerges purely from induced slash directions.
- **Semantic Hypothesis Space**:
  - Unary predicates: `kind:<v>`, `color:<v>`, `size:<v>`
  - Binary relations: `rel:<r>(x0,x1)`, `rel:<r>(x1,x0)`, `rel:<r>:sym(x0,x1)`
  - Speech-act marker: `Q`
- **MDL Scoring & Beam Search (`nexus/learner/nexus_learner.py`)**:
  - Prior: $\log P(h) = -\alpha \cdot (|L_h| + \sum_{e \in L_h} \text{depth}(e) + \sum_{e \in L_h} \text{atoms}(e))$ ($\alpha = 0.05$).
  - Likelihood: $\log P(y_i \mid s_i, w_i, h) = \log(1-\varepsilon)$ if $\text{eval}(\text{LF}_h(w_i), s_i) = y_i$ else $\log(\varepsilon)$ ($\varepsilon = 0.02$).
  - Beam of $K = 200$ joint hypotheses updated from True-vs-False scene contrasts; predictions via Bayesian model averaging over the beam; full JSONL experience log for `correct()` replay.
- **Explainability & Persistence (`nexus/learner/explain.py`, `nexus/storage/repository.py`)**:
  - `explain(sentence, scene)` returns the derivation tree, lexical entries with supporting example IDs, derived LF, object binding witness (or falsifying atom reason), and posterior weight.
  - `what_changed(example_id)` returns hypotheses promoted, demoted, or created.
  - `SQLiteLearnerRepository` persists and reloads the full learner state, beam, and provenance in SQLite (`nexus/storage/schema.sql`).
